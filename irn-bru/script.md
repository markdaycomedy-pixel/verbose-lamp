# Irn-Bru — gold-mosaic history explainer (draft v1)

Target: ~185 words, roughly 70 s at Mark's pace.

## Voiceover

1. **[Opening roundel: Mark, mid-shrug, holding a bottle]**
Scotland has two national drinks. One is whisky. The other is bright orange and tastes like a fairground swallowed a penny.

2. **[Falkirk, 1875]**
Eighteen seventy-five. Robert Barr, a cork cutter in Falkirk, decides the real money is in what's *under* the cork. He starts making fizzy water. Scotland, famously short of water, is delighted.

3. **[1901: Iron Brew]**
Nineteen-oh-one. The family launch "Iron Brew". It has iron in it. Actual iron. Ammonium ferric citrate, which is normally something you find holding up a bridge.

4. **[1946: the vowels go]**
Then, nineteen forty-six. New labelling rules. Slight problem: it was never brewed. So they kept the sound and binned the vowels. Irn-Bru. Legally distinct. Spiritually identical.

5. **[The secret recipe]**
The recipe is thirty-two flavours, supposedly known to two people who never travel together. Which is either corporate security or the dullest thriller ever written.

6. **[Girders]**
The slogan: "Made in Scotland from girders." Nobody checked. Nobody wanted to.

7. **[Beating Coca-Cola]**
For decades it outsold Coca-Cola in Scotland. One of the very few places on Earth where Atlanta lost.

8. **[2018 sugar panic]**
In twenty-eighteen they cut the sugar. People stockpiled it by the crate, like rationing was coming back.

9. **[Closing: Mark with the bottle]**
So. Irn-Bru. Orange. Slightly metal. Officially not a hangover cure. Unofficially, the only thing standing between Scotland and Monday. Slàinte.
